ML-Driven Creator Recommendations

Brands struggled to source creators for partnership ads, one of Instagram's highest-impact formats. I applied machine learning (ML) to creator-brand matching at scale, driving the MVP from concept to launch.

Challenges

Creator discovery was slow and subjective — it was hard to evaluate which creators truly aligned with a brand’s values.

In paid marketing, brands rely on niche creators for authentic, high-performing content. But the marketplace was a passive directory: 77% of searches returned no matches, and agencies spent up to 1.5 months sourcing creators by hand.

1

Creator content buried in the layout

For paid marketing, content quality matters. But the creator card was built around creator stats like follower count, location, and category, with no way to preview the content itself, leaving brands unable to evaluate what actually drives results.

2

Manual creator sourcing

Sourcing and evaluating creators was highly manual — often outsourced entirely to third-party agencies — making it the biggest pain point in running partnership ads efficiently.

3

Lack of visibility on brand partnerships

A creator's track record partnering with brands or posting branded content is a strong signal of how likely their content is to perform as a partnership ad. But the discovery experience didn't surface this history at all, making it hard for brands to gauge the probability that a creator's content would convert into a successful partnership ad.

Hypothesis

Leveraging Meta’s rich data to power ad-optimized, ML-driven recommendations would increase brand-creator matches.

I saw an opening: use Meta's ML to surface the creators whose content converts best — fueling the marketplace's pivot to partnership ads, Instagram's fastest-growing format.

Craft

Decisions that shaped the product

Designing the creator card for a two-second scan

I established four evaluation principles: ads relevance, scannability, feasibility, and scalability and prioritized a content-first layout that surfaced the most relevant work immediately. The design shipped, and the evaluation principles became the rubric for every creator card variant that followed.

Sequencing AI search out of the MVP

I proposed AI-powered search — describe the creator, then refine — as the long-term solution. Leadership backed it fast, but shipping it in the MVP would have pushed scope past the critical path, so I moved it to the next phase and documented the rationale. The MVP stayed focused, and the team aligned around AI search as the next strategic bet.

Actions as a training signal

Instead of introducing new interactions, I turned existing behaviors into implicit feedback signals. Among them, Not Interested became the highest-value signal. It helped brands quickly remove poor matches while continuously improving recommendation quality.

Solution

Closing the Personalization Loop

I designed the end-to-end discovery experience — the content-first surface, the signals it ranked on, and the “Not Interested” feedback loop — partnering with ML and Data Science, who built the model that powered it. That loop turned every dismissal into a training signal, continuously improving recommendations.

Reflections

What I Learned

Great ecosystems are grown, not built

Creator Portfolio drove rapid creator adoption, but brand discovery lagged. Two years later, ML-powered discovery finally closed the loop, increasing portfolio views by 20–72% and completing the two-sided ecosystem.

Ad performance ≠ content quality

A creator can meet every partnership ads criterion and still produce content that doesn't resonate. The real challenge was balancing ad conversions with authentic, high-quality content — too complex for MVP but essential for long-term trust.

Understanding the workflows shaped the strategy

Mapping out how brands and agencies actually sourced and vetted creators — the back-and-forth, the manual handoffs, the disconnected tools — revealed exactly where the matchmaking process broke down. That understanding directly shaped the strategy for streamlining it with ML.
